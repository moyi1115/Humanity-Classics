# Human Classics Reading

> A deep reading series of cross-civilizational classic works, integrating Zen koans, historical stories, and modern life practices

## Project Overview

This is a **Human Classics Reading** content project, aiming to provide deep readings of cross-civilizational classic works, combining Zen koans, historical stories, and modern life practices to explore the common wisdom of humanity.

**Civilizations Covered:** Buddhism, Taoism, Confucianism, Western Philosophy, Christianity, Islam, Hinduism

## Reading Framework

Each reading contains four core sections:

### Buddhist Classics

1. **Sutra Text** — Original canonical text
2. **Entering Prajna** — 3 subsections of deep interpretation
3. **Koan Highlights** — Zen koans resonating with the essence
4. **Modern Application** — Contemporary life practice

### Taoist Classics

1. **Original Text** — Original canonical text
2. **The Principle of Tao** — 3 subsections of deep interpretation
3. **Historical Mirror** — Historical stories resonating with the essence
4. **Life Wisdom** — Contemporary application

### Confucian Classics

1. **Original Text** — Original canonical text
2. **Words of the Sages** — 3 subsections of deep interpretation
3. **Dialogue Between Past and Present** — Historical figures compared with contemporary
4. **Self-Cultivation Practice** — Specific practical methods

### Western Philosophy

1. **Excerpts** — Key passages
2. **Philosopher's Thought** — 3 subsections of deep interpretation
3. **Echo of the Times** — Historical background and contemporary significance
4. **Reflection of Reality** — Modern life application

### Religious/Wisdom Literature

1. **Excerpts** — Key passages
2. **Words of Wisdom** — 3 subsections of deep interpretation
3. **Dialogue Between Past and Present** — Historical background and multiple interpretations
4. **Contemporary Revelation** — Practical methods for modern people

## Writing Characteristics

- **Thought Depth > Word Count**: 1500-2000 words per chapter, quality over quantity
- **Direct to the Core**: Every point is sharp, no encyclopedic explanations
- **Cross-Civilizational Dialogue**: Thought collisions between different classics and civilizations
- **Practice-Oriented**: Touching on real pain points of modern people (anxiety, identity attachment, desire for control, etc.)

## Great Fish Mentor

This project includes a **Great Fish Mentor (Wisdom)** skill that provides deep conversations based on classic reading materials.

### What is Great Fish Mentor?

Great Fish Mentor is a intellectual mentor based on 31 classic reading materials, helping users gain insights from classical wisdom in real-life dilemmas.

**Name Origin:** Taken from the movie "Big Fish & Begonia", also representing "great wisdom appears foolish, and 'da yu' (great fish) is a homophone of 'da yu' (great foolishness)".

### How to Use?

In Claude Code, type:

```
/wisdom
```

### Core Functions

- **More Than Retrieval, It's Understanding**: Understanding your dilemmas, building profound connections between multiple classics
- **More Than Answers, It's Inspiration**: Helping you gain your own insights and strength from classical wisdom
- **More Than Solutions, It's Growth**: Every conversation is an internal practice

### Memory System

Great Fish Mentor has memory function, remembering your conversation history and growth trajectory:

- **User Profile**: Recording your core values, common dilemmas, conversation preferences
- **Conversation History**: Recording key information from each conversation
- **Automatic Cleanup**: Automatic cleanup every 20 conversations to maintain conversation quality

### Use Cases

When encountering the following problems, you can have a conversation with Great Fish Mentor:

- Work anxiety, identity confusion
- Interpersonal relationship困惑
- Pursuit of meaning
- Confusion in spiritual practice
- Need for classical wisdom insights

### Example Conversation

**User:** I've been under a lot of work pressure lately, always feeling anxious.

**Great Fish Mentor:** I remember we discussed identity anxiety before... We found the wisdom of "dwelling nowhere" in the Diamond Sutra before, but this time you mentioned work pressure. This reminds me of what the Tao Te Ching says about "being startled by favor and disgrace" — we care too much about results and lose the peace in the process...
